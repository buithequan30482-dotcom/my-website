About Vân Đồn and Its Heritage Sites

The land of Vân Đồn

Vân Đồn Special Zone lies in the south-east of Quảng Ninh province and comprises more than 600 islands in Bái Tử Long Bay, next to Hạ Long Bay, considered the largest archipelago in northern Vietnam. Its total area is 2,171.33 km², of which 583.92 km² is land and 1,587.41 km² sea; its population of about 53,900 belongs to 17 ethnic groups. The name Vân Đồn is said to come from Vân mountain in the Quan Lạn area, “the mountain veiled in cloud”.

In 1149 King Lý Anh Tông established the Vân Đồn estate to open trade with foreign merchant ships, the beginning of Đại Việt's first trading port, a network of anchorages across many islands that operated for centuries. These waters also witnessed the 1288 Vân Đồn victory of the Trần army under Trần Khánh Dư. In modern times, Cẩm Phả district was established on 26 December 1948 and renamed Vân Đồn district on 13 March 1994; from 1 July 2025 all 12 communes and towns of the district merged to form Vân Đồn Special Zone with 72 hamlets and quarters. The zone has Vân Đồn International Airport, the Hạ Long – Vân Đồn Expressway and Ao Tiên high-grade port.

Heritage sites

According to the reviewed heritage inventory, Vân Đồn Special Zone has 44 heritage sites:

  • 1 Special National Monument: the Vân Đồn Trading Port Complex (listed in 2023), with sites on Thắng Lợi and Quan Lạn islands.
  • 3 National Monuments: the Quan Lạn Historical and Architectural Relic Cluster (Sao Ỏn, Đông Hồ and Cao Sơn shrines); the President Hồ Chí Minh Memorial Site on Ngọc Vừng Island; and the Ba Vũng Archaeological Site.
  • 7 Provincial Monuments: the Ngọc Vừng Militia 12.7 mm Anti-aircraft Position; Ngọc Vừng Communal House and Shrine; King Lý Anh Tông Temple and Đông Trong Cave; Ngọc Vừng Ancient Citadel; Khe Mai Resistance Base; the Mother Goddess Temple (Đền Mẫu); and Trấn Hải Temple (Đền Cậu).
  • 33 inventoried sites: prehistoric caves (Nhà Trò, Hoi, Soi Nhụ, Hà Giắt, Quan), communal houses, temples, shrines and pagodas in Bản Sen, Cái Rồng, Đoàn Kết, Minh Châu, Quan Lạn, Thắng Lợi, Hạ Long and Vạn Yên; ancient landings of the trading port (Cái Cổng, Con Quy, Cống Yên, Cống Hẹp, Cống Cái); scenic beaches; and revolutionary sites such as the place where an American pilot was captured, the Hồ Chí Minh Trail at Sea and the Kế Bào coal mine memorial site.

Beyond tangible heritage, the Quan Lạn Communal House Festival, commemorating the 1288 Vân Đồn victory, was inscribed on the National List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2019. The special zone is also home to Cặp Tiên Temple (Đông Xá area), part of the Cửa Ông Temple Special National Monument and managed by the Cửa Ông – Cặp Tiên Temple Management Board.

Layers of culture

Vân Đồn's heritage spans many periods: prehistoric sites such as Ba Vũng, Soi Nhụ and Đông Trong (the Institute of Archaeology counts more than ten prehistoric sites in Vân Đồn); the Lý, Trần and Lê trading-port era with its ancient landings, pagodas and towers; communal houses, shrines and temples honouring those who defended the sea; and sites of the resistance against the United States on Ngọc Vừng island. By type, the sites are classified as historical, architectural and artistic, archaeological, and scenic landscape.

About this website

This website was created by the Culture and Society Office of Vân Đồn Special Zone to introduce the area's heritage to residents and visitors in Vietnamese, English and Chinese. Its content is compiled from the heritage inventory and published sources (newspapers, government portals and research), with sources cited on each page. Some inventoried sites are still sparsely documented; we welcome comments, documents and photographs from readers to help complete the content.
