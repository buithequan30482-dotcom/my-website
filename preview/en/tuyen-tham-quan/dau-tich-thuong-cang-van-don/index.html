Traces of the Vân Đồn Trading Port

2 days, 1 night – Car (Cái Rồng – Ao Tiên); passenger boat Ao Tiên – Quan Lạn; electric cart, bicycle or motorbike on the island

A journey in search of Đại Việt's first trading port, founded in 1149. It begins at the temple of King Lý Anh Tông in Cái Rồng, the king who founded the Vân Đồn estate, then crosses to Quan Lạn island to visit Cái Làng landing, identified by many researchers as the heart of the port, along with the communal house, pagoda and shrines linked to the 1288 victory at Vân Đồn. It continues to Cống Cái landing on Quan Lạn and the ancient anchorages of Con Quy and Cái Cổng on Minh Châu, where archaeologists have found ceramics and coins spanning centuries.

Most of the old anchorages are now ruins: please do not dig or collect sherds. The Thắng Lợi island sites (Cống Đông – Cống Tây) require a separate boat; contact local people in advance.

Stops

  1. King Lý Anh Tông Temple and Đông Trong Cave

    Provincial Monument Cai Rong

    Start: the temple of the king who founded Vân Đồn in 1149.

  2. Vân Đồn Trading Port Complex

    Special National Monument Thang Loi

    Cái Làng landing, Hệu Well, Quan Lạn Communal House and Pagoda, Trần Khánh Dư Shrine; Thắng Lợi sites by separate boat.

  3. Cống Cái Landing

    Inventoried site Quan Lan

    Cống Cái ancient landing, where excavations revealed wharf remains, stone embankments and layers of ceramics.

  4. Con Quy Landing

    Inventoried site Minh Chau

    Con Quy landing (Minh Châu), one of the port's earliest centres.

  5. Cái Cổng Landing

    Inventoried site Minh Chau

    Cái Cổng ancient landing (Minh Châu), now a ruin.

Dashed lines join the stops in order and do not show the actual route.
