Quan Lạn Mother Goddess Temple (Đền Mẫu)

Thôn Quan Lạn 1, Đặc khu Vân Đồn – Historical
Báo Quảng Ninh điện tử (bài: Phạm Học)

A temple to the Three Mother Goddesses on a spit of land jutting into the sea at the eastern gate of Quan Lạn island, preserving a royal decree of 1889 and several antiques. The Mother's anniversary falls on the 3rd day of the 3rd lunar month. Provincial monument since 2023.

History

The Mother Goddess Temple (Đền Mẫu) stands in former Yến Hải hamlet (now Quan Lạn 1 hamlet) on Quan Lạn island. Because it sits at the island's north-eastern sea gate, it is also called the Eastern Gate Mother Temple (Đền Mẫu cửa Đông); its official name is the Temple of the Three Mother Goddesses (Đền thờ Tam tòa Thánh Mẫu).

Its exact founding date is unknown. Local tradition holds that it dates from the 13th century, like Trấn Hải Temple. Based on its artefacts and the 1889 royal decree, researchers believe it was built no earlier than the 15th–16th centuries and no later than the 19th. The provincial inventory records that 14th-century bowl fragments and 15th- and 17th–18th-century vase and jar fragments have been found around the temple.

It began as a small shrine with bamboo walls and a thatched roof and was rebuilt in wood under the Nguyễn. Damaged during the war against the French, it was rebuilt in brick after 1954, then almost flattened by American bombs in 1968, leaving only the rear sanctuary of about 8 m². It was restored in 2010 and rebuilt in 2021 with donations from people within and beyond the province.

According to Quảng Ninh Newspaper (15/10/2023), under the Nguyễn dynasty the temple had a timber frame with earthen walls and an earthen floor; when it was rebuilt in brick after 1954, it was roofed with traditional yin-yang tiles. The 2010 restoration was funded by donations from the people of Yến Hải hamlet themselves, and at that time the temple grounds covered only about 1,900 m². The 2021 reconstruction was carried out on the old temple foundation, with contributions from organisations and individuals from inside and outside the province.

Beliefs

The principal deity is the Mother of Heaven (Mẫu Thượng Thiên, the Holy Mother Liễu Hạnh); later, villagers added the Mother of the Mountains and Forests (Mẫu Thượng Ngàn) and the Mother of the Waters (Mẫu Thoải), forming the Three Mother Goddesses. Mother Goddess worship in a seafaring village such as Quan Lạn reflects the hope for protection from storms, calm seas and safe voyages.

According to the article “Mother Goddess worship in Quảng Ninh” in Quảng Ninh Newspaper (05/03/2023), the Mother temple in Yến Hải hamlet also contains a place of worship for Cậu Bé Cửa Đông (the Young Prince of the Eastern Gate), a figure whose origins and life story remain unclear. The article groups this shrine with other temples linked to the worship of the Water Mother (Mẫu Thoải) by seafaring communities in Vân Đồn, alongside Cặp Tiên temple (Đông Xá commune), which honours Cô bé Cửa Suốt. Another Quảng Ninh Newspaper article (16/02/2025) on water-deity worship also mentions the Quan Lạn temple of the Three Holy Mothers, including the Water Mother, as an expression of the cult of river and sea deities in Quảng Ninh.

Architecture and setting

The temple stands on a raised spit of land surrounded by sea, known locally as “thoi đền Mẫu”, facing south-south-west. The grounds now cover 5,617.88 m², with a main temple on a T-shaped plan, a courtyard, a votive-paper burner, a guesthouse and an offering-preparation house. Black Chinese characters on the wall read “Đông Hải linh từ” (Sacred Temple of the Eastern Sea), and a statue of the White-Robed Guanyin Crossing the Sea stands at the front right.

The temple preserves an original royal decree from the first year of Emperor Thành Thái (1889) honouring the Holy Mother Liễu Hạnh, together with nine antiques: a bronze incense burner, a bronze bell, three porcelain incense bowls, a ceramic lime pot, two ceramic candlesticks and a stoneware vase, all considered representative examples of craftsmanship.

Festival

The Mother's anniversary is held every year on the 3rd day of the 3rd lunar month. Before 1959 there was also a boat race, held about two months after the Quan Lạn Communal House rowing festival, smaller in scale and limited to the village. The course was a closed loop of about 1,600 m, starting at Đền Mẫu, rounding Trấn Hải Temple and returning to Đền Mẫu for the prize. Two teams, Giáp Vạn Giữa and Giáp Vạn Ngoài, each crewed a boat with a commander and 15–19 rowers paddling to the beat of a drum. The race has not been held since 1960 and has not yet been revived.

Significance

Đền Mẫu is a representative site of Mother Goddess worship among the islanders of Quan Lạn, part of the cultural landscape of the old Vân Đồn trading port. On 18 January 2023 the Quảng Ninh People's Committee issued Decision No. 168/QĐ-UBND listing it as a provincial historical monument.

According to Quảng Ninh Newspaper (28/09/2022), the scientific dossier of the Mother temple was reviewed by the provincial Council for Appraising Scientific Dossiers for the Ranking of Historical-Cultural Relics and Scenic Sites at its meeting on 28 September 2022, chaired by Ms Nguyễn Thị Hạnh, Vice Chairwoman of the Provincial People’s Committee and Chair of the Council. Before the meeting, the Department of Culture and Sports – the Council’s standing body – had verified the information and documents in the dossier. The Council agreed to rank the Mother temple as a provincial-level historical relic and asked the Department to finalise the dossier for submission to the Provincial People’s Committee. At the same meeting, the Council also reviewed the dossiers of Trấn Hải temple (also in Yến Hải hamlet) and of the French colonial political prison relic in Hòn Gai.

Visitor information

According to the introduction page of the Angsana Quan Lạn resort (a travel source), the Mother temple lies on the way to Eo Gió in the south of Quan Lạn island and is a worthwhile stop for visitors interested in history. According to VnExpress (19/7/2024), Eo Gió is close to the Cậu temple at Cửa Đông, about 5 km south of the Quan Lạn ferry pier; electric carts are available for getting around the island.

Visitors combining the temple with Eo Gió should take care: according to the Vân Đồn e-portal (29/4/2026), the Eo Gió area in Yến Hải hamlet has steep terrain and lacks tourism safety infrastructure, and the authorities have requested that warning signs be installed in coordination with the hamlet head and forest owners.

Visitor guide

Take a passenger boat from Ao Tiên Port to Quan Lạn harbour, then an electric cart or motorbike to the eastern gate area. The visit combines well with Trấn Hải Temple and Eo Gió. Boat schedules vary with season and weather; check with the port for the latest timetable.

The temple sits on a spit by the sea: watch the tide and waves when walking along the shore and avoid visiting during bad-weather warnings. Inside, please dress modestly and keep quiet.
