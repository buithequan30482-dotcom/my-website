Quan Lạn Relic Cluster: Sao Ỏn, Đông Hồ and Cao Sơn Shrines

Thôn Quan Lạn 1, thôn Quan Lạn 2, Đặc khu Vân Đồn – Historical – Architectural and Artistic
Cổng TTĐT đặc khu Vân Đồn

A national historical and architectural relic cluster on Quan Lạn island, listed in 1990 and linked to the three Phạm brothers, venerated for their role in the 1288 Battle of Vân Đồn. The communal house, pagoda, Đức Ông Shrine and Trần Khánh Dư Shrine now belong to the Vân Đồn Trading Port Complex.

History

The Quan Lạn Historical and Architectural Relic Cluster was recognised as a national monument by the Ministry of Culture and Information under Decision No. 575 of 14 July 1990. It originally comprised the communal house, pagoda, shrines and the nghè (a small temple) at the centre of the island commune, together with the shrines of the three Phạm brothers. When the Vân Đồn Trading Port Complex was listed as a Special National Monument in 2023, the communal house, pagoda, the shrine of the Phạm brothers (Đức Ông Shrine) and the Trần Khánh Dư Shrine were transferred to that complex; according to the current inventory, this national cluster now consists of three sites: Sao Ỏn Shrine (Miếu Sao Ỏn), Đông Hồ Shrine (Miếu Đông Hồ) and Cao Sơn Shrine (Miếu Cao Sơn).

Quan Lạn island covers about 15 km² and is some 20 km long, narrowing to about 100 m at its slimmest point. It was once known as Làng Vân, Làng Cả or Cái Làng; one hypothesis holds that the name Quan Lạn derives from “Quang Lạn”, two Sino-Vietnamese characters meaning radiant light, a name still found on an old pair of parallel sentences in the communal house. Before 1945 Quan Lạn belonged to Vân Hải canton, Cẩm Phả district, Quảng Yên province. The old village was divided into six giáp (Đông, Nam, Đoài, Bắc, Văn, Võ); every hamlet had a watch post and alarm drum, and men aged 18 to 50 took turns standing guard.

Legends and beliefs

The Sao Ỏn, Đông Hồ and Đức Ông shrines are linked to the three Phạm brothers: Phạm Công Chính, Phạm Quý Công and Phạm Thuần Dụng. Tradition holds that these sons of the sea helped Trần Khánh Dư attack the Yuan supply fleet commanded by Trương Văn Hổ, hiding their boats in the fog to lie in ambush. Folk memory gives them homely nicknames: “Ông Nhòm”, the keen-eyed lookout who spotted enemy ships from afar; “Ông Hô”, who raised the alarm and rallied the islanders; and “Ông Đục”, the strong diver who holed the enemy's boats. In notes from 1967, Prof. Trần Quốc Vượng recorded seeing the brothers' ancestral tablet in Quan Lạn Communal House.

All three fell in the naval battle. Phạm Công Chính's body washed up on the southern sand spit where Đức Ông Shrine now stands; Phạm Thuần Dụng's came ashore nearly 3 km away, at the site of Đông Hồ Shrine (also written Đồng Hồ); and Phạm Quý Công's drifted to the foot of Sao Ỏn hill beside the Mang River, where Sao Ỏn Shrine was built. Sources differ on which brother is honoured at Sao Ỏn and which at Đông Hồ. Research by Hoàng Giáp and colleagues suggests the brothers were deputy generals of Trần Khánh Dư, originally from Khê Chanh village (Quảng Yên), where the Phạm clan also keeps their tablets.

No published material describing Cao Sơn Shrine specifically has yet been collected; worship of the Cao Sơn mountain deity is also recorded at the Vân Sơn temple-and-shrine complex in Sơn Hào hamlet of the same commune.

According to Quang Ninh Newspaper (article “On the three Phạm generals worshipped in Quan Lạn”, 2013), Sao Ỏn shrine stands in Tân Phong hamlet and Đông Hồ shrine in Thái Hòa hamlet of the former Quan Lạn commune. The Vân Đồn Special Zone e-portal adds that Đồng Hồ shrine lies about 200 m from Đồng Lĩnh dune. Quang Ninh Newspaper (2012) notes that Quan Lạn pagoda and the shrines of the three Phạm brothers follow Nguyễn-dynasty architecture and are built mainly of brick. Besides the three individual shrines, according to Quang Ninh Newspaper (2016), tablets of the three generals are also worshipped together in the temple beside the communal house, within the grounds of Quan Lạn pagoda.

Related buildings in the Quan Lạn setting

The three shrines share a cultural landscape with Quan Lạn Communal House (Đình Quan Lạn), an imposing late-19th-century building on an I-shaped plan with 32 main and 26 secondary wooden columns, a fish-scale tile roof, a ridge decorated with two dragons facing the moon, and a timber floor; it honours King Lý Anh Tông, the village guardian deity and the founding ancestors. Beside it stands Linh Quang Pagoda, dedicated to the Buddha, the Mother Goddess Liễu Hạnh and Cụ Hậu, a local woman who sold her land to repair the pagoda. Đức Ông Shrine, dedicated to Phạm Công Chính and bearing the inscription “Hải bất dương ba” (may the sea raise no waves), was rebuilt in 2001 and restored in 2023. About 1.5 km from the communal house is the Trần Khánh Dư Shrine, rebuilt on a large scale in 2011.

According to Quang Ninh Newspaper (article “Đức Ông shrine in Quan Lạn”, 2023, by Phạm Học), Đức Ông shrine was originally a thatched structure, later rebuilt on a larger scale and renovated around the late Lê – early Nguyễn period; it is regarded as the centre of the Vân Đồn Traditional Festival. The 2023 restoration ran from February to July, cost over 10 billion VND raised from public contributions and was carried out by Tâm Đức Company; inside the shrine is a relief depicting the naval battle on the Mang River. The travel site Sun Paradise Land (secondary source) describes the shrine as built of ironwood (lim) and mần lái wood, in a 丁-shaped (chữ Đinh) plan, facing the sea at Đình wharf.

On the Trần Khánh Dư shrine (nghè), an article by Phan Thị Thúy Vân (Quang Ninh Museum) in Quang Ninh Newspaper (2023) states that it stands in Thái Hòa hamlet; Trần Khánh Dư is worshipped there as tutelary god, while Quan Lạn communal house worships him by proxy. The nghè was rebuilt in 2011 at a total cost of about 28 billion VND, partly funded by the national target programme. The complex comprises the main shrine, left and right side halls, a shrine to the Four Holy Ladies (Tứ vị Thánh Nương), a half-moon pond and gardens; its doors and interior are of ironwood carved in Trần-dynasty style, and it keeps many artefacts from the Later Lê and Nguyễn periods. The Special Zone e-portal adds that the old nghè was a small 一-shaped (chữ Nhất) building that fell into ruin in the 1960s and was repaired in 1995.

On Quan Lạn communal house, Vietnam Law Newspaper (2021) records a different dating: the house was first built in the Later Lê period (17th century) at Cái Làng wharf, then moved to Nam hamlet, and only in the 12th year of Thành Thái was it moved to its present site in Đoài hamlet. It is built of mần lái wood; besides dragon carvings it bears images of silkworms and mantis shrimp, reflecting the islanders’ sericulture and fishing; three mountains rise in front of it, one of them Sao Ơn (Sao Ỏn).

Festival

The Phạm brothers are honoured during the Quan Lạn Communal House Festival (the Vân Đồn traditional festival), a national intangible cultural heritage held from the 10th to the 19th day of the 6th lunar month, with the main day on the 18th. Before the rowing race between the Đông Nam Văn and Đoài Bắc Võ teams, the two teams' “generals” make offerings at Đức Ông Shrine.

According to Quang Ninh Newspaper, the 2025 Vân Đồn Traditional Festival included the procession of royal decrees, offerings to the deities at the communal house, shrines and nghè, a ritual for the earth god, the “thu rầm” ritual, the appointment of generals and a parade on the Mang River; the festivities featured the performance “The Vân Đồn battle formation” in the communal house yard. According to Thương hiệu và Công luận magazine, the 2026 festival runs from 23 July to 1 August 2026 (10th–19th day of the 6th lunar month), with the main rituals from 29 July to 1 August (16th–19th) at Quan Lạn communal house, Đức Ông shrine, the nghè of Nhân Huệ Vương Trần Khánh Dư and Trấn Hải temple; the main festival day is 31 July 2026 (18th day of the 6th lunar month), combined with the ceremony announcing Trấn Hải temple’s ranking as a provincial relic. The rituals listed include raising flags and “locking the village” to open the festival, receiving the dragon boats, the deity procession, “thu rầm”, appointing generals, withdrawing troops to camp, returning the palanquin to the sanctuary, and praying for peace.

Significance

The shrines of the Phạm brothers bear witness to the beliefs of a seafaring village and preserve the community's memory of the 13th-century resistance against the Mongol-Yuan invasions and of the islanders' role in defending the north-eastern seas. According to the inventory, Đông Hồ Shrine has been restored.

Visitor information

Sun Paradise Land (secondary source) advises modest dress, bringing offerings such as fresh flowers, incense and fruit, and asking beforehand about burning votive paper at the shrines; early morning or late afternoon visits are best, avoiding the storm season. The communal house, pagoda and Đức Ông shrine stand side by side at Đình wharf and can be visited together; it is quiet on ordinary days and very crowded during the festival in the 6th lunar month.

Visitor guide

Take a passenger boat from Ao Tiên Port to Quan Lạn harbour; on the island, electric carts, bicycles or motorbikes reach the commune centre and the shrines. Sao Ỏn Shrine lies near the Mang River; Đông Hồ Shrine is a few kilometres from the village centre. Boat schedules vary with season and weather; check with the port for the latest timetable.

These are active places of worship: please dress modestly, keep your voice down and ask the caretaker before photographing inside. The 6th-lunar-month festival draws large crowds, so book accommodation early.
