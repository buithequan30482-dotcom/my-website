Ngọc Vừng Ancient Citadel

Thôn Ngọc Vừng, Đặc khu Vân Đồn – Historical
Afamily (ảnh: Q.L.)

A coastal defence post beside Trường Chinh beach on Ngọc Vừng island. Nguyễn-dynasty records state it was built in 1839 as Tĩnh Hải fort, though locals long called it the Mạc citadel. Only traces of one wall section remain.

History

The Ngọc Vừng Ancient Citadel stands beside Trường Chinh beach in former Bình Ngọc hamlet on Ngọc Vừng island. It was a coastal watch fort, part of the defence system of the north-eastern seas that protected the Vân Đồn trading port and controlled the sea lane past Ngọc Vừng, west of Quan Lạn island. According to the Quảng Ninh newspaper, the Lý, Trần, Lê, Mạc and Nguyễn dynasties all regarded Ngọc Vừng as a key post guarding the northern maritime frontier.

There are two views on the citadel's date. The site dossier prepared by the Quảng Ninh Museum in 2011 holds that it was built under the Mạc (16th century), hence the popular name “Mạc citadel”, and rebuilt in 1839. Nguyễn-dynasty records, however, name it Tĩnh Hải fort (Bảo Tĩnh Hải), built in the 20th year of Emperor Minh Mệnh (1839) and associated with Nguyễn Công Trứ. Researcher Nguyễn Cảnh Loan rejects the Mạc theory, noting that the 2011 dossier relied on the recollections of two elderly villagers born in the early 20th century and that no Mạc-era written source mentions the fort; he proposes calling it the “Ngọc Vừng Ancient Citadel” or the “Nguyễn citadel”. The provincial inventory uses the name Ngọc Vừng Ancient Citadel.

On the role of Nguyễn Công Trứ, articles in Afamily (May 2026) and Người Quan Sát (June 2026) report that in 1839 Governor-General Nguyễn Công Trứ petitioned the court to reinforce the citadel, add troops and supply two more cannons. Citing the Đại Nam nhất thống chí, both articles add that the post was permanently manned by one commander (quản vệ), 150 soldiers and three large warships. According to a study by Nguyễn Minh Đại on the Nguyễn dynasty’s coastal defence system (published on the Nghiên cứu Lịch sử website, 2021), Emperor Minh Mệnh had the Tĩnh Hải and Ninh Hải posts built in Quảng Yên in 1839; in 1851 Emperor Tự Đức abolished the three posts of Nhiếp Hải, Ninh Hải and Tĩnh Hải, leaving the local district to assign soldiers for patrols and reconnaissance. By this account, the post served as an imperial garrison for only a little over a decade.

Regarding the “Mạc citadel” theory, Báo Quảng Ninh (April 2025) writes that under Emperor Trần Dụ Tông (1349) a garrison was set up on the north-west of Ngọc Vừng island to detect and resist northern invaders, and that the Mạc dynasty (1527–1595) sent troops to the island to build the Cửa Mai citadel against pirates. The article does not say whether Cửa Mai is the same as the citadel by Trường Chinh beach. Another Báo Quảng Ninh article (April 2025), by contrast, describes Ngọc Vừng as “where the Nguyễn dynasty placed the Tĩnh Hải citadel”.

Structure according to the records

According to the Đại Nam thực lục (Veritable Records of Đại Nam), each side measured 23 trượng; the foundation was 1 thước 5 tấc deep and the wall 5 thước high. The outer face was of stone 2 thước 5 tấc thick, the core of earth 3 thước 5 tấc thick, and the inner face of stone 2 thước thick (the trượng, thước and tấc being traditional units of length). There were front and rear gates, each framed by wooden posts about 9 thước tall, with a watchtower above and double doors below. A bastion shaped to the terrain stood on the left, and a beacon platform on the right. The Đại Nam nhất thống chí (Gazetteer of Đại Nam) gives a perimeter of 134 trượng 8 thước and a height of 5 thước.

According to Báo Quảng Ninh (December 2021), the Tĩnh Hải post in Vựng hamlet, Ngọc Vừng commune, had a gun emplacement holding four guns, two of them cannons, together with a beacon tower and a watch post; the post was built of stone and earth. The article also reports that in 2007 local people found a bronze cannon in Ngọc Vừng commune, identified as made in Spain in 1870; it does not say whether this gun belonged to the Tĩnh Hải post. Nguyễn Minh Đại’s 2021 study groups small watch posts and forts such as those on Cô Tô and Ngọc Vừng among Nguyễn-era works influenced by Vauban-style military architecture, combined with Vietnamese fortification traditions and built from local materials.

On its size, an Afamily article (May 2026) converts the figures in the Đại Nam nhất thống chí into modern units: a square citadel about 130 m per side, about 2 m high, with a rampart top about 4 m wide. These are the writer’s conversions, not field measurements.

Archaeology

A 1973 excavation showed an earthen core faced with stone set in lime mortar; the walls were one course of stone thick and the corners two, each course about 0.5 m. Two gates about 3 m wide opened in the middle of the walls: the north gate for sorties or for blocking attackers coming up from the Cổng Đồn river, and the south gate, facing the sea, as the main defensive point against seaborne attack. Finds included porcelain bowls and dishes and fragments of jars, all from the Lê and Nguyễn periods, with no Mạc-period ceramics.

According to Afamily (May 2026) and the Du lịch Ngọc Vừng website, the 1973 survey was carried out by a team from the Institute of Archaeology. Báo Quảng Ninh (November 2017) reports that in 2017 researcher Nguyễn Cảnh Loan re-surveyed the citadel within its designated protection zone; the article was illustrated with plan, cross-section and elevation drawings of the citadel and a photograph of a surviving wall stone.

Setting

The citadel lies by Trường Chinh beach, a crescent of fine white sand nearly 3 km long on the island's south side. At the end of the beach is Pháo Đài islet, a small wooded island of about 0.42 ha; according to the Quảng Ninh newspaper it once served soldiers as a lookout for pirate ships, which probably explains its name, meaning “fort”.

Significance

The citadel is considered one of the rare relatively intact coastal military forts in northern Vietnam, evidence of successive dynasties' maritime defence policies. It was listed as a provincial monument by Decision No. 3921/QĐ-UBND of 13 December 2011, covering 30,000 m². Today only traces of one wall section and a displayed wall stone remain; Nguyễn Cảnh Loan has proposed reconstructing Tĩnh Hải fort according to the historical descriptions as a visitor attraction.

Current condition and visiting

According to Afamily (31 May 2026) and Người Quan Sát (3 June 2026), only a few low, worn sections of stone revetment remain, overgrown with weeds, right at the edge of Trường Chinh beach; Người Quan Sát attributes the heavy damage to wartime bombing and sea storms. A stele marking the remains of the ancient citadel stands at the site. There is no ticket gate, no street vendors and no loudspeaker commentary. The Du lịch Ngọc Vừng website places the citadel in the Cái Tặc area of Bình Hải hamlet.

Báo Quảng Ninh (August 2021) reports that the commune has demarcated a protection zone for the citadel and that restoration has been proposed for conservation and tourism. A Quảng Ninh TV report (April 2021) observed that the site seemed forgotten and that its cultural and historical value had not yet been developed for tourism.

To get there, according to Afamily, Trường Chinh beach is about a 15-minute walk from the island centre and the citadel lies right beside it; visitors can rent a bicycle or electric cart along the concrete road across the island and are advised to come early in the morning.

Visitor guide

Take a passenger boat from Ao Tiên Port to Cống Yên landing (Ngọc Vừng), then an electric cart or motorbike toward Trường Chinh beach on the south of the island. A visit pairs well with swimming or watching the sunrise at the beach. Boat schedules vary with season and weather; check with the port for the latest timetable.

Little of the walls survives; please do not climb on or remove stones from them.
