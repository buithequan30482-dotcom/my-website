Ngọc Vừng Beach

Thôn Ngọc Vừng, Đặc khu Vân Đồn – Scenic Landscape
Ảnh minh họa do hệ thống tạo – cần thay ảnh chính thức

A crescent of fine white sand nearly 3 km long on the south of Ngọc Vừng Island, known as Trường Chinh Beach; Pháo Đài islet lies at its far end.

LandscapeThe nameThings to do PhotosReviews

Landscape

Ngọc Vừng Beach was inventoried as a scenic site in 1996 and covers about 70,000 m². The island's finest beach is Trường Chinh Beach, right next to the centre of the island commune: a crescent of fine white sand nearly 3 km long, separated from the village by a casuarina forest. At its far end lies Pháo Đài ("Fortress") islet, about 0.42 ha; according to Quảng Ninh Newspaper, historical records say it was once a lookout post of the Mạc dynasty's troops for spotting pirate ships.

The name

In 1962 General Secretary Trường Chinh visited the island, and Ngọc Vừng's most beautiful beach was named after him.

Things to do

Besides swimming, visitors can watch the sunrise on the beach, rake for clams and scoop small shrimp with local fishermen, or row out to the rocky islets to gather snails. Islanders use the surrounding rocks and islets to farm snails, fish and clams. The island also has the Hồ Chí Minh Memorial Site, the Ngọc Vừng communal house and shrine, the Ngọc Vừng Ancient Citadel and other sites to combine with a beach visit.

Visitor guide

Take a high-speed island ferry from Ao Tiên Port (the hub for all passenger boats to Vân Đồn's islands since 28 February 2023) to Ngọc Vừng Island; Cống Yên pier on the north of the island is now its main passenger pier.

Trường Chinh Beach is right next to the centre of the island commune.

Swimming safety

  • Swim only in permitted areas with signage and lifeguards, and follow the beach management's instructions.
  • Do not swim far out, in high waves, strong wind, thunderstorms or at night; do not swim after drinking alcohol or right after a heavy meal.
  • Always watch children; non-swimmers should use a buoyancy aid.
  • Watch out for rocky reefs, sharp objects and marine creatures in shallow water.
  • Follow weather forecasts and local authority notices, especially in the storm season.

Check the boat schedule and weather forecast before setting out, especially in the rainy and storm season; services may be suspended in bad weather.
