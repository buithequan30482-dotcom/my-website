Vạ Giếng Temple Ruins (Vụng Giếng)

Thôn Thắng Lợi, Đặc khu Vân Đồn – Historical
Báo Quảng Ninh (bài: Nguyễn Trung Dũng)

Ruins on Cống Đông island (Thắng Lợi), recorded in the heritage list as a Trần-dynasty temple to Hưng Đạo Đại Vương Trần Quốc Tuấn; local people have built a small shrine on the old foundation.

LocationRemainsLink with the trading port PhotosReviews

Location

The site is on Cống Đông island, facing Cống Tây island, in the former Thắng Lợi commune; its foundation lies right at the water's edge. The heritage inventory (1996, about 2,000 m²) records it as a Trần-dynasty temple dedicated to Hưng Đạo Đại Vương Trần Quốc Tuấn, now in ruins.

Remains

According to an article by Nguyễn Trung Dũng of the Quảng Ninh Key Heritage Sites Management Board (2012) – which calls the site Vạ Giếng Pagoda – only the foundation remains; local people have built a small shrine directly on it and still worship there. Beneath the shrine are column bases, green-stone architectural elements, fragments of mythical-animal decorations, pieces of a terracotta tower model and a turned stone element about 0.5 m tall carved with lotus petals. Judging by its architectural and decorative style, the author believes the structure existed at least from the Lê – Mạc period and proposed an archaeological excavation for study and restoration.

Link with the trading port

Cống Đông – Cống Tây was part of the Vân Đồn trading port system and now lies within the Vân Đồn Trading Port Complex (VD01).

According to the same 2012 article by Nguyễn Trung Dũng in Quảng Ninh Newspaper, Cống Đông island was once the seat of Nghiêu Phong district under the Nguyễn dynasty; a joint Vietnamese–Japanese archaeological survey in 2002 recovered ceramics dating from roughly the 14th–16th centuries there. The author considers the surviving foundations at Vạ Giếng to belong to a structure older than the island's Nguyễn-era history.

Visitor guide

The former island commune of Thắng Lợi (now Thắng Lợi village) is served by passenger boats from Ao Tiên Port. Sites are scattered across the islands and around the coves, so ask the local authorities or residents for directions before setting out.

This is a ruin with no visitor facilities or on-site guiding. Please do not dig or collect artefacts (ceramic sherds, bricks, stones, etc.) – all artefacts at heritage sites are protected by law.
