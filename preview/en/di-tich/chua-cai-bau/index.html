Cái Bầu Pagoda

Khu phố Hạ Long 1, Đặc khu Vân Đồn – Historical
Ảnh minh họa do hệ thống tạo – cần thay ảnh chính thức

The Trúc Lâm Giác Tâm Zen Monastery on Bái Tử Long Bay, built on the site of the Trần-dynasty Phúc Linh Pagoda, with a 50-metre Buddha statue on the hilltop and sweeping views over the bay.

History

Cái Bầu Pagoda, officially Trúc Lâm Giác Tâm Zen Monastery, stands right on Bái Tử Long Bay near the Bãi Dài tourist area, in the former Hạ Long commune (now Hạ Long 1 quarter, Vân Đồn Special Zone). According to Quảng Ninh Newspaper, it was built on the site of Phúc Linh Pagoda, which dates from the Trần dynasty some 700 years ago. The old pagoda was badly damaged and modestly restored in the 1990s. Construction of the present monastery began in 2007 and it was inaugurated in 2009, funded by public donations.

Architecture and setting

The complex includes a two-storey main hall, a three-entrance gate, a patriarchs' hall, bell and drum towers, a meditation hall, guest houses and monks' quarters. The main hall houses a statue of Shakyamuni Buddha flanked by the Bodhisattvas Manjushri and Samantabhadra, with carved panels telling the story of the Buddha's path. On the hilltop behind stands a 50-metre Buddha statue. From the monastery courtyard, nearly 100 m above sea level, visitors can take in the whole of Bái Tử Long Bay.

Festival

The festival season runs from the 1st to the 3rd lunar month, when crowds of locals and visitors come to worship, enjoy the spring and pray for peace.

Significance

Cái Bầu Pagoda is Vân Đồn's best-known spiritual destination, linked to the Trúc Lâm Zen tradition and to the foundations of an ancient Trần-dynasty pagoda on Bái Tử Long Bay.

Visitor guide

The site is on Cái Bầu Island, Vân Đồn's largest island, which is reached by road. From Hạ Long, take the Hạ Long – Vân Đồn expressway; Vân Đồn International Airport is also located in the special zone.

The pagoda is about 10 km from central Cái Rồng and about 65 km from central Hạ Long City. According to Quảng Ninh Newspaper (2020): entry is free, you can walk up to the pagoda, private vehicles can park in the open lot beside it, there are no touts or stalls in the grounds, and it can be visited all year round, busiest in the early-year festival season.

At places of worship, please dress modestly, keep quiet and respect local religious practices.
