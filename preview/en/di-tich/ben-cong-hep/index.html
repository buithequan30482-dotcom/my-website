Cống Hẹp Landing

Thôn Ngọc Vừng, Đặc khu Vân Đồn – Historical
Photo of the surrounding area · Dân Việt (ảnh: QH)

An ancient landing on the north of Ngọc Vừng Island which, together with Cống Yên, was a busy harbour of the Vân Đồn trading port system; now in ruins.

Cống Hẹp Landing is on Ngọc Vừng Island; it was inventoried in 1996 and covers about 20,000 m². The heritage list records it as an ancient Lý-dynasty landing linked to the Vân Đồn trading port, now in ruins.

Within the port system

According to Quảng Ninh Newspaper, Cống Hẹp and Cống Yên were the two landings on the north of Ngọc Vừng, busy and important harbours of the Vân Đồn trading port from the Lý to the Nguyễn dynasty. The Vân Đồn trading port was established in 1149 under King Lý Anh Tông; it was not a single harbour but a system of landing places spread across many islands of Bái Tử Long Bay. Cống Hẹp is not among the components of the Vân Đồn Trading Port Complex (VD01) classified as a Special National Monument.

No archaeological study dedicated to Cống Hẹp has been published, and its position in the project's data is approximate.

According to Báo Quảng Ninh (15/08/2021), the Cống Hẹp area on Ngọc Vừng island still preserves traces of the ancient Vân Đồn trading port. A study by Đinh Thị Thanh Nga (Institute of Archaeology) in the journal Khảo cổ học (Archaeology), no. 2/2023, also names Cống Hẹp – alongside Cống Yên, Mang Thúng, Cổng Chào and Vụng Sim – among the landing places of the Vân Đồn trading-port system in Vân Đồn district, without describing specific artefacts. VietNam News (2020) presents Cống Yên and Cống Hẹp as two historical sites on the north of the island that visitors can explore.

Visitor guide

Take a high-speed island ferry from Ao Tiên Port (the hub for all passenger boats to Vân Đồn's islands since 28 February 2023) to Ngọc Vừng Island; Cống Yên pier on the north of the island is now its main passenger pier.

This is a ruin with no visitor facilities or on-site guiding. Please do not dig or collect artefacts (ceramic sherds, bricks, stones, etc.) – all artefacts at heritage sites are protected by law.
