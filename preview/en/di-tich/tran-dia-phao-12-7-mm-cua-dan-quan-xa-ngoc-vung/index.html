Provincial Monument

Ngọc Vừng Militia 12.7 mm Anti-aircraft Position

Thôn Ngọc Vừng, Đặc khu Vân Đồn – Historical
Ảnh minh họa do hệ thống tạo – cần thay ảnh chính thức

On Điếm Canh hill on 24 December 1972, the Ngọc Vừng militia platoon, armed with three 12.7 mm anti-aircraft machine guns, shot down the 200th American aircraft over Quảng Ninh. A provincial monument since 2002.

History

Ngọc Vừng is a small island commune in a forward position on the north-eastern seas. Since the Trần dynasty a watch post called Điếm Canh stood on the island's north-west to keep watch over the sea, a name that survives in the hill at the centre of the commune today. During the American air war, the island absorbed 1,588 bombs and 1,697 rockets, about two-thirds of the total dropped on the other communes of Cẩm Phả district (today Vân Đồn). Cống Yên, the island's landing, was a key point for receiving supplies and was heavily bombed. That spirit had deep roots: on 27 September 1945 the young people of Ngọc Vừng, together with Việt Minh representatives, seized power and set up the commune's provisional revolutionary committee.

In 1967 the Cẩm Phả district military command formed the Ngọc Vừng militia platoon, equipped with three 12.7 mm anti-aircraft machine guns. The position was set up on Điếm Canh hill, about 107 m above sea level near the commune centre, with dug-in emplacements and careful camouflage. The platoon leader was Nguyễn Thanh Sửu, later Party Secretary and Chairman of Cô Tô district. Besides the 12.7 mm position, 14.5 mm and 37 mm guns were scattered across the island to intercept aircraft.

Feats of arms

From 1965 to 1968 the island's forces fought 231 engagements and shot down 14 aircraft. The air battle over Ngọc Vừng peaked in late December 1972: on the 19th the commune's forces hit three aircraft, one of which crashed off Quan Lạn; on the 23rd another caught fire and fell on Trường Chinh beach.

On 24 December 1972, from the position on Điếm Canh hill, the 12.7 mm crews fired a long burst that brought down an F-4, the 200th American aircraft downed over Quảng Ninh. The pilot parachuted into the sea and was captured. Over both phases of the war, Ngọc Vừng's soldiers and people shot down 23 American aircraft and captured or killed dozens of pilots. In 1973 the commune was named a Hero of the People's Armed Forces.

The people of the position

Memories of the position live on in eyewitness accounts. Nguyễn Xuân Thu, a gunner in the second crew, was among those who fought there. Behind the guns were the island's women and young people: Nguyễn Thị Lan, former secretary of the commune Youth Union, led seven young women supporting the fighting, while Nguyễn Thị Hoan was one of seven youths who carried and tended the wounded. Each story shows the islanders' resolve to hold the island and hold their positions.

Significance

The Ngọc Vừng militia's 12.7 mm position is one of the emblematic sites of the resistance war against the United States in Quảng Ninh, alongside the Xuân Sơn 12.7 mm position in Đông Triều, which shot down the 100th aircraft in 1966. It was listed as a provincial monument by Decision No. 4121/QĐ-UB of 14 November 2002, covering 8,827 m². From the hilltop, visitors can take in a panorama of the island commune and understand how such a small island earned the title of hero.

Visitor guide

Take a passenger boat from Ao Tiên Port to Cống Yên landing (Ngọc Vừng), then an electric cart or motorbike to the commune centre; Điếm Canh hill is close by. It combines easily in one day with the Hồ Chí Minh Memorial, the Ngọc Vừng communal house and shrine, and the old citadel. Boat schedules vary with season and weather; check with the port for the latest timetable.

The path up the hill is steep in places: wear walking shoes, bring water, and avoid heavy rain or the midday sun.
