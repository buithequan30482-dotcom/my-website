Hang Nhà Trò Cave

Thôn Bản Sen 1, Đặc khu Vân Đồn – Scenic Landscape
Photo of the surrounding area · Cổng TTĐT Đặc khu Vân Đồn – Khám phá Bản Sen (Ngân Hà, 20/04/2026)

A limestone cave on Bản Sen Island, inventoried as a scenic landscape in 1996. The heritage list records Stone Age traces and an unspoilt natural cave landscape.

OverviewSignificanceBản Sen in contextVisitor notesNote on sources PhotosReviews

Overview

Hang Nhà Trò Cave lies in the former commune of Bản Sen (now Bản Sen 1 village, Vân Đồn Special Zone). In the zone's heritage list it is classified as a scenic landscape, inventoried in 1996, with a protected area of about 215,000 m².

Significance

The heritage list records traces from the Stone Age in the cave, which is also a natural cave landscape. In an article introducing Bản Sen, Quảng Ninh Newspaper counted Nhà Trò among the island's beautiful caves – still wild and little known.

According to Quảng Ninh Newspaper (the article “Tapping the potential of caves”, 2025), within the cave system of Bái Tử Long Bay, Nhà Trò lies a little farther from the mainland than Đông Trong Cave, in the territory of the former Bản Sen commune; the cave has a pristine beauty with many oddly shaped stalactite formations. The Vân Đồn Special Zone e-portal (April 2026) likewise singles out Nhà Trò as the most remarkable of the island's caves, its stalactite layers almost intact because of limited human impact. According to Quảng Ninh Newspaper (June 2026), Trà Bản Island has only a few limestone caves, the largest of which is Nhà Trò.

Bản Sen in context

Bản Sen is one of Vân Đồn's island communes, known for local produce such as Bản Sen oranges and Vân tea. Besides Nhà Trò, the Quyết Tiến area (former Đồng Gianh village) has several other caves, which are almost completely dark inside.

According to Quảng Ninh Newspaper (June 2026), the island locals call Bản Sen is officially named Trà Bản. Under French rule it was called Ile de la Table (“the table”); on 23 July 1968, Decision No. 372-NV of the Ministry of the Interior of the Democratic Republic of Vietnam officially renamed it Trà Bản, while Bản Sen was originally the name of a village on the island. The Vân Đồn Special Zone e-portal notes that Bản Sen is one of the few earthen islands among the limestone islands of Bái Tử Long, about 12 km from Cái Rồng port, and is known as the “oyster capital” of Vân Đồn.

Visitor notes

According to Quảng Ninh Newspaper, visitors exploring the caves of Bản Sen should bring a flashlight, as there is almost no light inside, and ideally hire a local guide. The Vân Đồn Special Zone e-portal suggests renting a motorbike or bicycle to get around the island. The exact location of the cave entrance has not been fixed in the current records, so it is advisable to ask village authorities or local residents before setting out.

According to Báo Quảng Ninh (29 June 2025), in 2024 Quảng Ninh Province announced 10 sightseeing routes on Bái Tử Long Bay, with Route 1 including Nhà Trò Cave; several routes linking Hạ Long Bay and Bái Tử Long Bay also include Nhà Trò Cave (Bản Sen). The article also notes that many caves in Vân Đồn do not yet meet safety conditions for visitors, so travellers should follow the announced routes with local guidance.

Note on sources

Detailed archaeological findings from the cave have not been published in the official sources available to us. This page will be updated when scientific records from the competent authorities become available.

Visitor guide

Take an island ferry from Ao Tiên Port (the hub for all passenger boats to Vân Đồn's islands since 28 February 2023) to Bản Sen. According to Quảng Ninh Newspaper (2015), motorbikes and tuk-tuks can be hired near the pier; accommodation and food services for visitors are limited, so plan ahead.

Quảng Ninh Newspaper's advice for Bản Sen's caves: they are almost completely dark, so bring a torch and preferably hire a local guide. Do not enter alone, and do not touch or break the formations.

Check the boat schedule and weather forecast before setting out, especially in the rainy and storm season; services may be suspended in bad weather.
