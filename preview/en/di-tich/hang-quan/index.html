Hang Quan Cave

Thôn Thắng Lợi, Đặc khu Vân Đồn – Scenic Landscape
Photo of the surrounding area · Báo Quảng Ninh – Du lịch "bụi" ở đảo Thắng Lợi (Công Thành, 27/10/2019)

A cave in the former Thắng Lợi commune, inventoried as a scenic landscape in 1996; the heritage list records Stone Age traces and its use as a mooring and repair base for naval vessels.

Hang Quan Cave is in the former Thắng Lợi commune (now Thắng Lợi village, Vân Đồn Special Zone). It was inventoried in 1996 as a scenic landscape and covers about 5,000 m².

Significance

According to the heritage list, the cave contains Stone Age traces and was once used as a mooring and repair base for naval vessels; it remains intact. Thắng Lợi covers the Cống Đông – Cống Tây islands, an area rich in heritage that includes many components of the Vân Đồn Trading Port Complex.

No scientific publication or official article dedicated to Hang Quan has been found.

According to Nhân Dân newspaper (27/11/2024), Hang Quan cave in the Cống Tây area was one of the strategic bases in the bay and once a secret naval harbour of the Vietnam People's Navy, from which torpedo boats set out to attack the US destroyer Maddox in August 1964. Quảng Ninh newspaper (27/10/2019) reports that many traces of the wartime naval unit remain in the cave; it is managed by the commune and seen as having potential for cave tourism.

Visitor guide

The cave is associated with naval activities, and there is no information on public access. Do not approach it on your own; consult the local authorities first.

The former island commune of Thắng Lợi (now Thắng Lợi village) is served by passenger boats from Ao Tiên Port. Sites are scattered across the islands and around the coves, so ask the local authorities or residents for directions before setting out.
