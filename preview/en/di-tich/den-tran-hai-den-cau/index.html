Provincial Monument

Trấn Hải Temple (Đền Cậu)

Thôn Quan Lạn 1, Đặc khu Vân Đồn – Historical
Ảnh minh họa do hệ thống tạo – cần thay ảnh chính thức

A temple in former Yến Hải hamlet on Quan Lạn island honouring Hoài Văn Hầu Trần Quốc Toản, the young Trần-dynasty general; restored in 2007. In the past it marked the turning point of the Yến Hải village boat race.

History

Trấn Hải Temple, popularly known as Đền Cậu (the Young Lord's Temple), stands in former Yến Hải hamlet (now Quan Lạn 1 hamlet) on Quan Lạn island. The name “Trấn Hải” means “guarding the sea”, fitting for a temple at the island's eastern sea gate, in an area bound up with the history of the Vân Đồn trading port and the naval battles against the Mongol-Yuan invaders.

According to local accounts recorded by the Quảng Ninh newspaper, Trấn Hải Temple was built at the same time as the nearby Eastern Gate Mother Temple. The provincial heritage inventory states that it was restored in 2007 and comprises a main temple, an offering-preparation house and a votive-paper burner; the site covers about 5,680 m².

Beliefs

According to the inventory, Trấn Hải Temple honours Hoài Văn Hầu Trần Quốc Toản, a member of the Trần royal family in the reign of King Trần Nhân Tông. History and tradition relate that at the Bình Than Conference in late 1282, when the court debated how to resist the invaders, Trần Quốc Toản was considered too young to take part. Undaunted, he raised his own troops and made a red banner embroidered with six golden characters, “Phá cường địch, báo hoàng ân” (Crush the mighty foe, repay the king's grace), then fought in the second resistance war against the Mongol-Yuan in 1285, in the mountains and alongside Trần Nhật Duật's army against Sogetu's forces on the river. The image of this young hero has entered school textbooks and the imagination of generations of Vietnamese.

That the people of Quan Lạn built a temple to a Trần general fits the island's wider pattern of worship: Trần Khánh Dư is honoured as guardian deity at the Quan Lạn nghè and communal house, and the three Phạm brothers at the Đức Ông, Sao Ỏn and Đông Hồ shrines. The popular name “Đền Cậu” also evokes the image of a youthful deity in local belief.

Festival

Trấn Hải Temple was once part of the Yến Hải village boat race. According to records on the Mother Goddess Temple, before 1959 the village held a race about two months after the Quan Lạn Communal House rowing festival; the closed course of about 1,600 m started at Đền Mẫu, rounded Trấn Hải Temple and returned to Đền Mẫu for the prize. Two teams, Giáp Vạn Giữa and Giáp Vạn Ngoài, rowed to the beat of a drum, each boat with a commander and 15–19 rowers. The race has not been held since 1960.

Setting

According to VnExpress, the Đền Cậu area lies near Eo Gió and Cửa Đông, well-known viewpoints on Quan Lạn looking out over Bái Tử Long Bay. Visitors often combine the temple with a tour of the island's east side.

Significance

Together with the Mother Goddess Temple, Trấn Hải Temple forms a religious cluster at the eastern gate of Quan Lạn, preserving the memory of lapsed community traditions such as the Yến Hải boat race. It is also where islanders express their reverence for the heroes of the Trần dynasty, figures linked to Vân Đồn's long history of guarding the sea. Here visitors can learn about the worship of Trần-dynasty generals while taking in the wide open seascape of the eastern gate.

Visitor guide

Take a passenger boat from Ao Tiên Port to Quan Lạn harbour, then an electric cart or motorbike toward Eo Gió – Cửa Đông. The nearby Mother Goddess Temple can be visited on the same trip. Boat schedules vary with season and weather; check with the port for the latest timetable.

Please dress modestly and keep quiet in the temple; at Eo Gió, beware of strong winds and keep back from the rock edges.
