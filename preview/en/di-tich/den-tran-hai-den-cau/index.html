Trấn Hải Temple (Đền Cậu)

Thôn Quan Lạn 1, Đặc khu Vân Đồn – Historical
Báo Quảng Ninh điện tử (ảnh: Ngân Hà, Đỗ Hiền – TT Truyền thông và Văn hóa đặc khu Vân Đồn)

A temple in former Yến Hải hamlet on Quan Lạn island honouring Hoài Văn Hầu Trần Quốc Toản, the young Trần-dynasty general; restored in 2007. In the past it marked the turning point of the Yến Hải village boat race.

History

Trấn Hải Temple, popularly known as Đền Cậu (the Young Lord's Temple), stands in former Yến Hải hamlet (now Quan Lạn 1 hamlet) on Quan Lạn island. The name “Trấn Hải” means “guarding the sea”, fitting for a temple at the island's eastern sea gate, in an area bound up with the history of the Vân Đồn trading port and the naval battles against the Mongol-Yuan invaders.

According to local accounts recorded by the Quảng Ninh newspaper, Trấn Hải Temple was built at the same time as the nearby Eastern Gate Mother Temple. The provincial heritage inventory states that it was restored in 2007 and comprises a main temple, an offering-preparation house and a votive-paper burner; the site covers about 5,680 m².

The road to official ranking for Trấn Hải Temple took several years. According to Quảng Ninh Newspaper, on 28 September 2022 the Quảng Ninh Provincial Council for Reviewing Scientific Dossiers on the Ranking of Historical-Cultural Relics and Scenic Sites, chaired by Vice Chairwoman of the Provincial People's Committee Nguyễn Thị Hạnh, examined the dossier for the “Trấn Hải Temple historical relic, Yến Hải hamlet, Quan Lạn commune, Vân Đồn district”. At that session the Council agreed to provincial ranking only for the nearby Mẫu Temple; for Trấn Hải Temple, the agencies concerned were asked to complete the dossier quickly and add historical data and legends on a proper legal basis to make the case more convincing.

Almost four years later, Quảng Ninh Newspaper reports, on 2 June 2026 the Quảng Ninh Provincial People's Committee issued Decision No. 1908/QĐ-UBND ranking Trấn Hải Temple as a provincial-level historical relic. The newspaper notes that the decision affirms the historical, cultural and religious value of the temple – a place long bound up with the spiritual life of Quan Lạn's seafaring community – and gives the locality a basis for conserving, restoring and embellishing it, linking the relic with cultural and spiritual tourism and heritage education.

Beliefs

According to the inventory, Trấn Hải Temple honours Hoài Văn Hầu Trần Quốc Toản, a member of the Trần royal family in the reign of King Trần Nhân Tông. History and tradition relate that at the Bình Than Conference in late 1282, when the court debated how to resist the invaders, Trần Quốc Toản was considered too young to take part. Undaunted, he raised his own troops and made a red banner embroidered with six golden characters, “Phá cường địch, báo hoàng ân” (Crush the mighty foe, repay the king's grace), then fought in the second resistance war against the Mongol-Yuan in 1285, in the mountains and alongside Trần Nhật Duật's army against Sogetu's forces on the river. The image of this young hero has entered school textbooks and the imagination of generations of Vietnamese.

That the people of Quan Lạn built a temple to a Trần general fits the island's wider pattern of worship: Trần Khánh Dư is honoured as guardian deity at the Quan Lạn nghè and communal house, and the three Phạm brothers at the Đức Ông, Sao Ỏn and Đông Hồ shrines. The popular name “Đền Cậu” also evokes the image of a youthful deity in local belief.

Some travel websites record a different popular understanding of the deity worshipped here. According to mia.vn (a secondary source), the “Cậu Temple at Cửa Đông” is a small temple dedicated to “Cậu Bé” (the Young Lord), whom local people regard as a nephew of Trần Hưng Đạo and believe protects fishermen on their voyages. This does not match the relic inventory (which names Hoài Văn Hầu Trần Quốc Toản) and has not been confirmed by official sources, so it should be treated as folk tradition awaiting verification.

Festival

Trấn Hải Temple was once part of the Yến Hải village boat race. According to records on the Mother Goddess Temple, before 1959 the village held a race about two months after the Quan Lạn Communal House rowing festival; the closed course of about 1,600 m started at Đền Mẫu, rounded Trấn Hải Temple and returned to Đền Mẫu for the prize. Two teams, Giáp Vạn Giữa and Giáp Vạn Ngoài, rowed to the beat of a drum, each boat with a commander and 15–19 rowers. The race has not been held since 1960.

Receiving the provincial relic certificate (2026)

According to Quảng Ninh Newspaper, on the evening of 29 July 2026, on Quan Lạn Island, the Vân Đồn Special Zone held a ceremony announcing the decision and receiving the certificate ranking Trấn Hải Temple as a provincial-level historical relic, combined with the opening of the 2026 Vân Đồn Traditional Festival – marking the 738th anniversary of the Vân Đồn Victory (1288). Vice Chairman of the Provincial People's Committee Nguyễn Văn Công presented the certificate, and Cao Tường Huy, Party Secretary and Chairman of the Vân Đồn Special Zone People's Council, beat the drum to open the festival.

According to the plan compiled by Thương hiệu và Công luận magazine, the 2026 Vân Đồn Traditional Festival ran from 23 July to 1 August 2026 (the 10th–19th days of the sixth lunar month), with the main festival day on 31 July (18th day of the sixth lunar month), organised at special-zone level; Trấn Hải Temple (Quan Lạn 1 hamlet) was one of the venues, together with Quan Lạn communal house, Đức Ông shrine and the shrine of Nhân Huệ Vương Trần Khánh Dư. The article also links the temple's historical value with the Vân Đồn Victory.

Afterwards, Quảng Ninh Newspaper (13 September 2026) quoted Deputy Chairman Đào Văn Vũ describing the successful Vân Đồn Festival and the ranking of Trấn Hải Temple as a milestone, after which the special zone would focus on improving its signature tourism products through the end of the year.

Setting

According to VnExpress, the Đền Cậu area lies near Eo Gió and Cửa Đông, well-known viewpoints on Quan Lạn looking out over Bái Tử Long Bay. Visitors often combine the temple with a tour of the island's east side.

According to mia.vn and VinWonders (travel sources), the temple sits quietly in a grove of trees in the Cửa Đông area – the far end of Quan Lạn Island – near Eo Gió, about 6 km from the ferry pier (VnExpress gives roughly 5 km to the south). Visitors can combine a visit to the temple with a climb up Eo Gió for a panoramic view of the island and the sea.

Significance

Together with the Mother Goddess Temple, Trấn Hải Temple forms a religious cluster at the eastern gate of Quan Lạn, preserving the memory of lapsed community traditions such as the Yến Hải boat race. It is also where islanders express their reverence for the heroes of the Trần dynasty, figures linked to Vân Đồn's long history of guarding the sea. Here visitors can learn about the worship of Trần-dynasty generals while taking in the wide open seascape of the eastern gate.

Visitor guide

Take a passenger boat from Ao Tiên Port to Quan Lạn harbour, then an electric cart or motorbike toward Eo Gió – Cửa Đông. The nearby Mother Goddess Temple can be visited on the same trip. Boat schedules vary with season and weather; check with the port for the latest timetable.

Please dress modestly and keep quiet in the temple; at Eo Gió, beware of strong winds and keep back from the rock edges.
