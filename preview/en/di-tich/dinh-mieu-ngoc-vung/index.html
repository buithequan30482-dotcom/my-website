Ngọc Vừng Communal House and Shrine

Thôn Ngọc Vừng, Đặc khu Vân Đồn – Historical
Ảnh minh họa do hệ thống tạo – cần thay ảnh chính thức

An 18th-century place of worship honouring the three Phạm brothers, venerated as village guardian deities for their role in the 1288 Battle of Vân Đồn. Its rite on the third day of Tết was revived in 2024 after more than 60 years.

History

The Ngọc Vừng Communal House and Shrine were built in the 18th century, during the Later Lê period, on the island commune of Ngọc Vừng. They form the shared place of worship of a seafaring community: the đình (communal house), where the village met and held ceremonies, and the miếu (shrine), where the deities are worshipped. On 19 June 2019 the Quảng Ninh People's Committee issued Decision No. 2470/QĐ-UBND listing the site as a provincial historical monument, covering more than 10,300 m².

Ngọc Vừng has long been bound up with guarding the sea. According to the Quảng Ninh newspaper, dynasties from the Lý to the Nguyễn regarded the island as a key post watching over the northern maritime frontier. In 1709 Ngọc Vừng belonged to Vân Đồn sub-prefecture, Quảng Yên garrison; under the Nguyễn it became a village of Vân Hải canton. The communal house and shrine were thus not only places of worship but a spiritual anchor for people living at the edge of the waves.

Beliefs

The site honours the three Phạm brothers: Phạm Quý Công, Phạm Công Chính and Phạm Thuần Dụng. According to legend and local records, they helped Trần Khánh Dư destroy the supply fleet of the Yuan general Trương Văn Hổ in the 1288 Battle of Vân Đồn, contributing to the victory at Bạch Đằng. The Nguyễn dynasty conferred royal titles on them, and the villagers venerate them as guardian deities (thành hoàng).

Worship of the Phạm brothers is widespread across Vân Đồn's islands: Quan Lạn has the Đức Ông, Sao Ỏn and Đông Hồ shrines; according to the heritage inventory, Bản Sen Communal House honours General Phạm Công Chính and Minh Châu Communal House the three Phạm brothers. Beyond Vân Đồn, the Phạm clan of Khê Chanh village (Quảng Yên) also keeps their tablets. This spread shows how deeply the memory of 1288 has entered the spiritual life of the whole archipelago, with Ngọc Vừng an important link in that cultural landscape.

Festival

In the past, every year on the third day of the Lunar New Year (Tết), the villagers of Ngọc Vừng held a communal-house rite to pray for national peace, favourable weather and bountiful harvests. Historical upheavals caused the rite to lapse for a long time. On the third day of Tết in the Year of the Dragon, 2024, the local authorities and people revived it; according to the Quảng Ninh newspaper, the procession of the deities had not been held for more than 60 years.

The revived rite included a procession from the shrine to the communal house with flag bearers, a traditional eight-instrument ensemble and offering bearers; a dragon palanquin (kiệu Long đình) carried by young village men; followed by drumming, three strikes of the bell, incense offerings and the reading of a prayer by the chief officiant. The solemn yet festive spirit of the first days of spring brought islanders and visitors back to the communal life of old.

Significance

The site holds deep spiritual value, preserving the memory of sons of the sea who fought invaders. Reviving the rite enriches the cultural identity of an island that once belonged to the Vân Đồn trading port system and offers visitors to Ngọc Vừng another cultural experience at the start of the year. Together with the Hồ Chí Minh Memorial, the anti-aircraft position on Điếm Canh hill and the old citadel, the communal house and shrine complete a journey through the island's long history.

Visitor guide

Take a passenger boat from Ao Tiên Port to Cống Yên landing, then an electric cart to the centre of Ngọc Vừng commune. Visitors wishing to attend the rite should come on the third day of the Lunar New Year and follow local announcements. Boat schedules vary with season and weather, especially around Tết; check with the port for the latest timetable.

Please dress modestly, remove hats, speak quietly inside and do not touch ritual objects.
