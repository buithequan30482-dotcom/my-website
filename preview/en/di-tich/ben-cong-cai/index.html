Cống Cái Landing

Thôn Quan Lạn 3, Đặc khu Vân Đồn – Historical
Báo Quảng Ninh điện tử (bài: Phan Thị Thúy Vân)

An ancient landing on Quan Lạn which, with Con Quy, was the earliest centre of the Vân Đồn trading port and again its centre in the final phase (17th–18th centuries); now an archaeological ruin.

Role in the port

Cống Cái Landing is in the former Quan Lạn commune (now Quan Lạn 3 village). It was inventoried in 1996 and covers about 45,000 m²; the heritage list records an ancient Lý-dynasty landing linked to the Vân Đồn trading port, now in ruins. According to the Journal of Archaeology (2023), Cống Cái was the centre of the port both in its formative phase (late 12th – early 13th century) and in its final phase (17th–18th centuries). Remains include traces of landing places, mooring stones, wells, 10th–13th-century Chinese glazed ceramics and a dense scatter of later stoneware jars.

According to the Journal of Archaeology (2023), Cống Cái lies in the central part of Quan Lạn Island and comprises the Sơn Hào valley and the Cống Cái – Mang River junction. It is a sheltered cove: its mouth is about 100 m wide at high tide and about 50 m at its narrowest, widening inside to a water surface of roughly 20,000 m². Over about ten years the site has undergone 8 surveys and 2 excavations. The Chinese ceramics recovered include white-glazed Ding ware (11th–13th centuries), brown-glazed ware close to the Guangxi kilns, and Jian ware (Fujian); glazed sherds of the 10th–13th centuries alone account for about half of all Chinese glazed sherds (610 of 1,208). There are also mid-13th-century Vietnamese celadon and stoneware in the Lý tradition.

The same study notes that at the end of the 13th century – after Trần Khánh Dư's battle – Cống Cái gradually lost its central role, losing it entirely by the late 14th – early 15th century, before becoming the main landing again in the 17th–18th centuries, when Revival Lê-period buildings stood around the foot of Đình Hill. Settlement in the Sơn Hào valley grew around the 13th–14th centuries, forming the old village of Sơn Hào. Báo Quảng Ninh (2022) reports scholars' view that the findings at Cống Cái – Sơn Hào reflect lively trade at Vân Đồn from the Lý period.

New discoveries

The Institute of Archaeology and the VMAP programme surveyed the area in 2012–2016; in April 2017 a team of Australian experts mapped the Cống Cái – Sơn Hào zone. A test pit on the south-western slope of Đình hill exposed a stone-retained platform from the Lê period with traces of the Trần period; the deepest layer is a Trần cultural layer resting on bedrock. Finds include metal objects, lead net sinkers, Trần and Lê coins and Qing coins. Researchers link the site with the 1288 Battle of Vân Đồn and General Trần Khánh Dư.

According to Báo Quảng Ninh (2017, an article by Phan Thị Thúy Vân of the provincial Relics and Scenic Sites Management Board), Cống Cái – Sơn Hào was excavated in 2016; during the survey of 13–22 April 2017 with a seven-member team from Australian institutions, the test pits also exposed traces of foundations, post holes and hearths, indicating at least two superimposed building floor layers.

Current state

In 2017 specialists recommended relocating a waste incinerator from the area and demarcating a protection zone for Cống Cái – Sơn Hào. Cống Cái is not among the components of the Vân Đồn Trading Port Complex (VD01) classified as a Special National Monument.

According to Vietnam News (September 2016), the Quảng Ninh Provincial People's Committee then agreed to operate a domestic-waste incinerator with a capacity of more than 10 tonnes per day, on a site of over 10,000 m² in Sơn Hào village, serving Quan Lạn and Minh Châu islands. Operation had earlier been halted after several experts objected because the incinerator stood on the ancient trading-port site; the province described the arrangement as temporary, saying the machine would be moved to the mainland in the following months. No source has been found confirming that the relocation was completed.

Visitor guide

The Cống Cái – Sơn Hào site is on Quan Lạn Island but has no visitor facilities or paths, and no coordinates in the project's data yet.

This is a ruin with no visitor facilities or on-site guiding. Please do not dig or collect artefacts (ceramic sherds, bricks, stones, etc.) – all artefacts at heritage sites are protected by law.
