Cống Yên Landing

Thôn Ngọc Vừng, Đặc khu Vân Đồn – Historical
Báo Quảng Ninh (bài: Tạ Quân)

A sheltered cove on the north of Ngọc Vừng Island that served as a "transit station" of the Vân Đồn trading port in the Lý – Trần period; the ancient landing is now a ruin, and a modern passenger pier stands on the old harbour area.

History

Cống Yên is a small sheltered cove on the north of Ngọc Vừng Island, close to the fishing grounds and well suited for anchoring and sheltering from storms. According to Quảng Ninh Newspaper, in the Lý – Trần period most trading vessels had to pass through Cống Yên before entering the heart of the port, so it was regarded as a "transit station" of the Vân Đồn trading port. Together with Cống Hẹp, it was one of two busy landings on the north of the island.

Through the ages

Under French colonial rule, the French built observation posts on the island to control the sea; in 1890–1891 Vietnamese insurgents set up a base against the French here. During the resistance war against the United States, the island's sea routes and landings helped ship weapons and supplies to the South.

According to Báo Quảng Ninh (29/04/2025), during the resistance war against the US, Cống Yên port – about 7 km from the commune centre – was the gathering point for boats carrying weapons and food aid from socialist countries, and it was therefore repeatedly attacked by US aircraft. On 3 May 1972, as boats carrying about 30 tonnes of rice and maize reached the port, US aircraft bombed it and fired rockets, killing many cadres and soldiers. On the afternoon of 3 August 1972 the port was bombed again: four people died and the warehouse caught fire; commune Youth Union secretary Nguyễn Thị Lan, seven young women and four soldiers carried the wounded to a first-aid station about 7 km away. On 18 December 1972 the port area was hit again during the B-52 raids, and on 24 December 1972 the body of the pilot of a just-downed aircraft was brought to Cống Yên port.

An archival photo that Báo Quảng Ninh (10/12/2023) reproduces from the History of the Ngọc Vừng Commune Party Committee shows residents welcoming First Secretary Lê Duẩn at Cống Yên port; according to Báo Quảng Ninh (29/04/2025), this visit to the island took place in June 1977.

Current state

The heritage list (inventoried in 1996, about 2,000 m²) records it as an ancient Lý-dynasty landing linked to the Vân Đồn trading port, now in ruins. A new passenger pier – the main gateway to Ngọc Vừng – now stands on the old harbour area. Cống Yên is not among the components of the Vân Đồn Trading Port Complex (VD01) classified as a Special National Monument.

According to Báo Quảng Ninh (15/08/2021), since 2018 the locality has allowed an investor to survey and restore the ancient Cống Yên port to serve visitors. Báo Quảng Ninh (10/12/2023) reports that the new pier built on the old port site has been included in the itineraries of many tours linking Hạ Long – Vân Đồn – Yên Tử. On the archaeological side, a study by Đinh Thị Thanh Nga (Institute of Archaeology) in the journal Khảo cổ học (Archaeology), no. 2/2023, lists Cống Yên among the landing places of the Vân Đồn trading-port system within Vân Đồn district, but does not describe any specific artefacts from the site.

Visitor guide

Take a high-speed island ferry from Ao Tiên Port (the hub for all passenger boats to Vân Đồn's islands since 28 February 2023) to Ngọc Vừng Island; Cống Yên pier on the north of the island is now its main passenger pier.

No standing structures of the ancient landing remain; visitors arriving here can take in the Cống Yên cove, the setting of the old harbour.

Check the boat schedule and weather forecast before setting out, especially in the rainy and storm season; services may be suspended in bad weather.
