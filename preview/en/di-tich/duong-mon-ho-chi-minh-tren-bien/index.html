Hồ Chí Minh Trail at Sea (Vạn Yên)

Thôn Vạn Yên 2, Đặc khu Vân Đồn – Historical
Ảnh minh họa do hệ thống tạo – cần thay ảnh chính thức

A site in Vạn Yên linked to the Hồ Chí Minh Trail at Sea, a departure point for the "ships without numbers" that supplied the southern battlefields; Vạn Hoa landing is where ship 154 set out in 1969.

The Hồ Chí Minh Trail at Sea

Group 759 was founded on 23 October 1961, opening a route that carried weapons from the North to the southern battlefields aboard the "ships without numbers" (tàu không số). Quảng Ninh, and especially the Hạ Long Bay area, served as a place to hide weapons, repair ships and launch many voyages.

Traces in Vân Đồn

The heritage list of the special zone records a site in the former Vạn Yên commune (about 10 ha, inventoried in 1996) as a departure point of the ships without numbers supplying the South. Quảng Ninh Newspaper also lists "the Hồ Chí Minh Trail at Sea in Vạn Yên commune" among the province's relics of the resistance war against the United States. According to veteran sailors, Vạn Hoa landing in Vân Đồn is where ship 154 set out at the time of President Hồ Chí Minh's death (1969); before leaving, the crew held a memorial service for him and pledged to complete their mission. The ship sailed a long detour through the waters of other Southeast Asian countries, changing flags to evade US reconnaissance aircraft, before entering the Vàm Lũng estuary in Cà Mau.

A Báo Quảng Ninh article (23/10/2023) adds details about the voyage of ship 154: it carried 58 tonnes of weapons; after leaving Vạn Hoa wharf it broke down and had to anchor near Sửng Sốt Cave in Hạ Long Bay for repairs, with the crew not allowed ashore. Once repaired, two Soviet ships brought men and weapons to the Sửng Sốt area, and the sailors transferred the weapons onto ship 154 by hand. The ship rounded the Lôi Châu (Leizhou) peninsula into international waters along the route previously taken by reconnaissance ship 42, carrying Chinese registration and flying the flag of whichever country it called at to avoid US aircraft. It then sailed south through Indonesian and Malaysian waters into the Gulf of Thailand, sighted the Hòn Khoai lighthouse at sunset and entered Vàm Lũng in heavy rain.

According to research by Dr. Trần Xuân Thanh and Assoc. Prof. Dr. Nguyễn Mạnh Dũng (Social Sciences Information Journal, No. 3/2023), Vạn Hoa port in Vạn Yên commune is the colonial-era Port-Wallut – a secondary transshipment hub on the Gulf of Tonkin built near the coal mines of eastern former Quảng Yên province. A 1900 prospectus of the Kế Bào company (compiled by the archive site entreprises-coloniales.fr) called it "the best natural harbour in Tonkin". Báo Quảng Ninh (28/6/2020) reports that the Vạn Hoa port area is now managed by a naval unit.

New records

On 6 October 2026, the Hồ Chí Minh Trail at Sea Veterans' Association presented Quảng Ninh Museum with a nautical chart marking the places in Quảng Ninh where the ships without numbers were prepared, hidden and repaired – material for exhibitions and for the dossiers of related heritage sites.

Visitor guide

The Vạn Hoa port area is currently managed by a naval unit and is not open to the public; the 10-ha site has not yet been mapped. Visitors interested in the ships without numbers can learn more at Quảng Ninh Museum in Hạ Long City.
