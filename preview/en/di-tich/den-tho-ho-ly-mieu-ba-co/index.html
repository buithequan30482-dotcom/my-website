Lý Family Temple (Ba Cô Shrine)

Khu phố Cái Rồng 3, Đặc khu Vân Đồn – Historical
Photo of the surrounding area · Báo Quảng Ninh điện tử (bài: Phạm Học)

The ruins of a local place of worship in Cái Rồng, recorded in the heritage list as founded in the Lý dynasty and connected with King Lý Anh Tông.

OverviewHistory and beliefsCurrent state PhotosReviews

Overview

The Lý Family Temple, also called Ba Cô Shrine ("Shrine of the Three Ladies"), is in the former Cái Rồng township (now Cái Rồng 3 quarter, Vân Đồn Special Zone). It was inventoried in 1996 and covers about 200 m².

History and beliefs

The heritage list records that the temple was founded in the Lý dynasty, belongs to local folk belief and is connected with King Lý Anh Tông – the king who established the Vân Đồn trading post in 1149 to receive foreign merchant ships. Cái Rồng is also home to the Temple of King Lý Anh Tông (Vân Hải linh từ), a provincial-level heritage site.

Current state

According to the list, only ruins remain. No published records describe the deities worshipped (the "Three Ladies") or the temple's detailed history.

Visitor guide

The site is a ruin in Cái Rồng 3 quarter (Cái Bầu Island, reachable by road). Visitors interested in King Lý Anh Tông can visit his temple in Cái Rồng.

This is a ruin with no visitor facilities or on-site guiding. Please do not dig or collect artefacts (ceramic sherds, bricks, stones, etc.) – all artefacts at heritage sites are protected by law.
