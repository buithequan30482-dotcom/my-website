Hạ Long Beach (Vân Đồn)

Khu phố Hạ Long 1, Đặc khu Vân Đồn – Scenic Landscape
Báo Quảng Ninh (bài: Phạm Tăng)

A beach on Bái Tử Long Bay in the former Hạ Long commune on Cái Bầu Island, inventoried as a scenic landscape in 1996. The area includes Bãi Dài (Long Beach), a popular seaside spot near Cái Bầu Pagoda.

Overview

Hạ Long Beach lies in the former Hạ Long commune (now Hạ Long 1 quarter, Vân Đồn Special Zone) on Cái Bầu Island, facing Bái Tử Long Bay. It was inventoried in 1996 as a scenic landscape.

According to VOV (2018), Bãi Dài lies on the large island of Cái Bầu, so it is closer to the mainland than the offshore beaches and can be reached by private vehicle. Together with Việt Mỹ and Mai Quyền, it is one of the island's best-known beaches, facing Bái Tử Long Bay, with accommodation and dining services right on the shore.

Landscape

The best-known beach in the area is Bãi Dài (Long Beach), about 3 km from Cái Bầu Pagoda. According to Quảng Ninh Newspaper (2016), the Bãi Dài tourist area covers more than 30 ha, with clear green water and flat white sand; guesthouses on the hill overlook the bay, and at the foot of the hill there are casuarina trees, lawns and a wooden bridge reaching out to sea. The area has been leased and developed since 2000 by Mai Quyền Tourism Co., Ltd; the province's 2018 list of tourist beaches names Mai Quyền (Bãi Dài) beach in Hạ Long commune as managed by this company.

Development of the resort

According to the Vietnam News Agency/VietnamPlus (2012), Mr Tạ Đức Quyết, head of the Mai Quyền Joint Venture Company, began investing in Bãi Dài in 1998. For about ten years the work was done largely by hand: cutting grass, clearing rubbish, adding white sand to the shore and planting trees, while avoiding large-scale levelling in order to preserve the landscape. By 2012 the company managed about 30 ha of coastal land for tourism and more than 100 ha of forest; the white-sand beach was over 1 km long, and the resort had 120 rooms, often fully booked from May to August. The article credited the project with helping to end sand mining and illegal logging and forest burning in the area.

According to Nhà đầu tư (Investor) magazine (2022), the area was originally swampy land with no electricity, water or infrastructure; the resort was completed at the end of 2006 and was one of the first tourist sites in Vân Đồn. According to Quảng Ninh Newspaper (2016), that year the company planned to upgrade the beach, rebuild the wooden bridge and open a 40-room four-star hotel with an outdoor pool, while its director stressed keeping Bãi Dài natural and unspoilt.

Things to do

Besides swimming, visitors can play beach volleyball, go fishing with local fishermen and try local seafood such as peanut worms (sá sùng), geoduck-like clams (tu hài), oysters and shrimp. Summer is the busiest season. Bãi Dài can be combined with a visit to nearby Cái Bầu Pagoda.

According to the travel sites VinWonders and mia.vn, the winding wooden bridge stretching out to sea is regarded as the symbol of Bãi Dài and a favourite photo spot for young visitors. At low tide the beach becomes wide and gently sloping, suitable for children and beach games; kayaking and jet-skiing are also offered. According to Vinpearl, the drive from central Hạ Long to Bãi Dài takes a little over an hour, and the best time to visit is April to June, when it is sunny with little rain.

Visitor guide

The site is on Cái Bầu Island, Vân Đồn's largest island, which is reached by road. From Hạ Long, take the Hạ Long – Vân Đồn expressway; Vân Đồn International Airport is also located in the special zone.

Bãi Dài is about 3 km from Cái Bầu Pagoda; according to VOV, it can be reached by private vehicle.

Swimming safety

  • Swim only in permitted areas with signage and lifeguards, and follow the beach management's instructions.
  • Do not swim far out, in high waves, strong wind, thunderstorms or at night; do not swim after drinking alcohol or right after a heavy meal.
  • Always watch children; non-swimmers should use a buoyancy aid.
  • Watch out for rocky reefs, sharp objects and marine creatures in shallow water.
  • Follow weather forecasts and local authority notices, especially in the storm season.
