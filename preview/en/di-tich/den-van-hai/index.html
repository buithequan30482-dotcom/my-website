Vân Hải Temple

Thôn Quan Lạn 3, Đặc khu Vân Đồn – Historical
Photo of the surrounding area · Báo Quảng Ninh

A temple on Quan Lạn Island dedicated to Trần Khánh Dư, the general associated with the Vân Đồn victory of 1288; according to the heritage inventory it was rebuilt in 1992.

Overview

Vân Hải Temple is in the former Quan Lạn commune (now Quan Lạn 3 village, Vân Đồn Special Zone). The heritage list states that it was rebuilt in 1992 and is dedicated to Trần Khánh Dư; it was inventoried in 1996 with a fairly large protected area of about 12,584.1 m².

Trần Khánh Dư and Quan Lạn

Trần Khánh Dư was a Trần-dynasty general associated with the Battle of Vân Đồn in 1288 during the resistance against the Mongol-Yuan invasion. On Quan Lạn he is worshipped in several places: in the Quan Lạn communal house and at the Trần Khánh Dư Shrine (nghè) in Thái Hòa village – the original shrine was built in the 18th century at Cái Làng landing and rebuilt on a large scale in 2011. During the Quan Lạn communal house festival (mid-6th lunar month), his memorial tablet is carried in procession from the shrine to the communal house and back.

Not to be confused

The name "Vân Hải" is easily confused with "Vân Hải linh từ", the formal Sino-Vietnamese name of the Temple of King Lý Anh Tông in Cái Rồng (a provincial-level site). Vân Hải Temple on Quan Lạn is a separate inventoried site dedicated to Trần Khánh Dư.

Details of the temple's architecture and artefacts are not yet available in published sources.

For comparison, according to an article by the Quảng Ninh Museum published in Quảng Ninh Newspaper (11 June 2023), the Nghè shrine to Trần Khánh Dư in Thái Hòa hamlet (part of the national relic complex ranked in 1990) has a three-bay, two-wing worship hall with four roofs covered in "shoe-tip" tiles and carvings imitating Trần-dynasty style, and still keeps many artefacts dating from the Later Lê and Nguyễn periods; its 2011 reconstruction cost about 28 billion VND in total, of which about 12 billion came from a national target programme. According to Pháp luật Việt Nam newspaper (2021), the Nghè lies about 1.5 km from Quan Lạn communal house. These features belong to the Nghè in Thái Hòa; no published source yet states whether Vân Hải Temple in Quan Lạn 3 hamlet is the same structure.

Quan Lạn Island also has the Trần Khánh Dư shrine (nghè) in former Thái Hòa hamlet, rebuilt on a large scale in 2011 (Báo Quảng Ninh, 11/06/2023), from which the royal decrees are carried to the communal house during the Vân Đồn Traditional Festival. In 2026 the festival was held at Trấn Hải temple (Quan Lạn 1 hamlet), the Quan Lạn Communal House area, Đức Ông shrine and the shrine of Prince Nhân Huệ Trần Khánh Dư; Trấn Hải temple received provincial relic status (Thương hiệu & Công luận, 10/07/2026). The name “Vân Hải linh từ” is also used for the King Lý Anh Tông temple in Vân Đồn (Báo Quảng Ninh, 30/05/2010), which is a different site. Before the August Revolution of 1945, Quan Lạn belonged to Vân Hải canton, Cẩm Phả district, Quảng Yên province (Báo Quảng Ninh, 05/06/2016).

Visitor guide

The temple's exact location is not yet in the project's data; ask residents of Quan Lạn 3 village for directions.

Take a high-speed island ferry from Ao Tiên Port (Bến cảng cao cấp Ao Tiên), the departure point for all passenger boats to Vân Đồn's islands since 28 February 2023. Boats to Quan Lạn Island call at either Quan Lạn port or Minh Châu port, on opposite sides of the island about 12 km apart; electric shuttle cars and motorbike rentals are available on the island.

At places of worship, please dress modestly, keep quiet and respect local religious practices.
