King Lý Anh Tông Temple and Đông Trong Cave

Khu phố Cái Rồng 4, Đặc khu Vân Đồn – Historical – Scenic Landscape
Báo Quảng Ninh điện tử (bài: Giang - Quân)

A temple in Cái Rồng honouring King Lý Anh Tông, who founded the Vân Đồn estate in 1149; it is said to date from 1172 and was rebuilt on its old foundations in 1993–1997. The listed site also includes Đông Trong Cave, a dwelling and burial place of the Hạ Long culture.

History

The temple of King Lý Anh Tông stands on the south-western slope of a hill near central Cái Rồng, today Cái Rồng 4 quarter of Vân Đồn Special Zone. Its formal name is “Vân Hải linh từ” (Sacred Temple of Vân Hải); the plaque at the centre of the gate reads “Hải Vân linh từ”. According to the Quảng Ninh newspaper, it was built in the 10th year of the Chính Long Bảo Ứng era (1172). Over time the temple fell into ruin, and from March 1993 to 1997 it was rebuilt on its old foundations.

In 2007 the Quảng Ninh People's Committee listed the “King Lý Anh Tông Temple and Đông Trong Cave” as a provincial historical and scenic monument by Decision No. 4426/QĐ-UBND of 28 November 2007, covering 102,000 m².

King Lý Anh Tông and Vân Đồn

King Lý Anh Tông, born Lý Thiên Tộ in the 4th month of 1136, was the eldest son of King Lý Thần Tông. In 1149, when merchant ships from Java, Lộ Lạc and Siam asked to settle and trade, he founded the Vân Đồn estate, the beginning of Đại Việt's first trading port. According to the Quảng Ninh newspaper, during a tour of the coast the king stopped at Cái Rồng, and local people preserved the spot where a thatched hut had received him.

Reverence for the king who opened the country to trade appears elsewhere in Vân Đồn too: Quan Lạn Communal House honours him, and on Đình hill at Cái Làng landing a small shrine holds a jackfruit-wood statue of him, which villagers used as the model in 1994 when recreating the statue in Quan Lạn Communal House.

The 1993–1997 rebuilding is recorded as having been funded by the South Korean businessman Lý Xương Căn, who identifies as a descendant of the Lý clan through Prince Lý Long Tường, who left for Goryeo in 1226. The article itself notes inconsistent generation counts, so these genealogical details have not been independently verified.

According to Báo Quảng Ninh (31/01/2023), King Lý Anh Tông came to Vân Đồn twice in his youth; in 1149, while touring the coast, he issued a decree establishing the Vân Đồn trading port, which went on to flourish for nearly five centuries. Local people built a shelter near the “mother dragon” mountain to receive him; after he left, they kept the spot as a memorial, which later became the temple.

Architecture and setting

The gate has a tiered roof, its ridge topped by a dragon facing the sun, and its body rests on four round pillars. A brick-paved path lined with trees leads up to it. The temple sits against the hillside as the ground rises; traditional geomantic descriptions speak of a long, high “dragon arm” and a short, low “tiger arm”, with a pavilion to the east and distant hills to the west forming a screen.

According to Báo Quảng Ninh (29/02/2012), the temple stands on Cái Rồng mountain near the Cái Rồng wharf and was rebuilt on its old foundations along the western slope; the pavilion on the east side is called Nghênh Phong (“Welcoming the Wind”). The work was carefully executed: floors paved with green Thanh Hóa stone, timber ceilings and red-lacquer-and-gilt finishes; the king’s altar rests on a dragon-carved Thanh Hóa stone base, with carved wooden furnishings, a raised ceiling and beams of precious wood; the worship hall has red-tiled floors, fronted by an imposing three-entrance gate and a spacious, open courtyard. The article notes that the authorities, businesses and benefactors jointly funded the restoration, with plans for a large forecourt linked to the road. Báo Quảng Ninh (05/05/2024) reports that the temple has been further upgraded in recent years.

Legend

The name Cái Rồng (“Mother Dragon”) comes from a legend: long ago, pirates coveting the sea's pearls, shrimp and fish raided the fishing villages. The Dragon King sent dragons and divine generals to help; the mother dragon turned herself into a mountain facing Cửa Suốt and directed the dragons, together with the unicorn, tortoise and phoenix, to whip up wind and waves until the pirates fled. The mountain where she stood became Cái Rồng mountain. During the wars against the Mongol-Yuan invaders, Cái Rồng was a place where Trần forces could regroup and preserve their strength, and Trần Khánh Dư once set an ambush here.

Festivals and religious life

According to Báo Quảng Ninh (08/02/2025), on the morning of the 11th day of the first lunar month of the year Ất Tỵ (8 February 2025), the Cái Rồng Township People’s Committee and the Management Board of the Temple of King Lý Anh Tông and Đông Trong Cave Scenic Site held the spring Prayer-for-Peace ceremony (Lễ Cầu an) at the temple, with offerings, incense, prayers, ritual sacrifice and the distribution of spring blessings, praying for favourable weather and peace for the nation and its people; Vân Đồn district leaders and large numbers of residents and visitors attended. The ceremony is held every year at the start of spring.

The temple is also tied to Vân Đồn’s Fishing Prayer Festival (Lễ hội Cầu ngư): according to Báo Quảng Ninh, at the second festival (9 February 2025) a ritual was held at the Temple of King Lý Anh Tông before the fishing-prayer rite at Cái Rồng fishing port; at the spring festival of Bính Ngọ (28 February 2026), a palanquin of King Lý Anh Tông was carried in procession from the temple to Cái Rồng fishing port and then back to the temple, alongside incense offerings, prayers for peace, a royal sacrifice and a communal feast.

Outside the major ceremonies, Báo Quảng Ninh (31/01/2023) notes that local people come to pray for peace on holidays and on the 1st and 15th days of each lunar month. Before the Giáp Thìn Lunar New Year, the paper (07/02/2024) reported that the temple was decorated, its flowers and ornamental plants tended, fire safety stepped up, and residents cleaned the access road to welcome visitors.

Đông Trong Cave

Đông Trong Cave is the scenic component of the listed site. Archaeological research places Đông Trong I and Đông Trong II among the cave sites of the Hạ Long culture (along with Bái Tử Long, Soi Nhụ and Trinh Nữ caves); Đông Trong II lies about 4 m above sea level. The cave served both as a dwelling and as a burial place for prehistoric people of the Hạ Long culture, at a time when communities were adapting to and exploiting the marine environment. The Quảng Ninh newspaper also lists Đông Trong among the scenic spots around Cái Rồng, alongside Đũa and Tỳ Nam islets.

According to the Báo Quảng Ninh report “Exploring Đông Trong Cave in Vân Đồn” (21/11/2023), the cave lies in Bái Tử Long Bay within the administrative area of the former Cái Rồng township; though less famous than the caves of Hạ Long Bay, it has its own appeal and preserves traces of Stone Age prehistoric habitation. Báo Quảng Ninh (07/04/2025) describes Đông Trong as the cave closest to the mainland in the Bái Tử Long cave system, with a wide chamber and a high entrance that make it easy for visitors to walk in and see the prehistoric remains. The paper’s 2012 article also notes that the cave is not far from the Temple of King Lý Anh Tông, making the two easy to combine on one route.

Significance

The site combines two layers of value: a temple commemorating the king who opened the Vân Đồn port, evoking an era when Đại Việt looked to the sea, and a cave bearing traces of prehistoric life. It is a fitting first stop for exploring Vân Đồn's history right in the centre of the special zone.

Visitor guide

According to Báo Quảng Ninh (29/02/2012), the temple sits conveniently on the sightseeing route, backed by the mountain and close to Cái Rồng port, so it is easy for visitors to reach; a visit can be combined with nearby Đông Trong Cave. Báo Quảng Ninh (31/01/2023 and 05/05/2024) suggests a spring pilgrimage route: coming from Cửa Ông Temple to Vân Đồn, visitors usually offer incense at Cặp Tiên Temple and Cái Bầu Pagoda before arriving at the Temple of King Lý Anh Tông. The sources consulted give no opening hours or admission fees.

Visitor guide

The temple is in Cái Rồng 4 quarter near the centre of the special zone, reachable by car or motorbike from Vân Đồn Airport or the Hạ Long – Vân Đồn Expressway. The path up has steep steps, so wear flat shoes. The location and access route of Đông Trong Cave have not been fully verified; ask the Special Zone's Culture and Society Office or local residents before going, and do not enter the cave without a guide.

At the temple, please dress modestly, keep quiet and place offerings only where designated.
